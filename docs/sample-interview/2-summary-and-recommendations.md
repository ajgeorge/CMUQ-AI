# Interview with Ahmed Al-Sayed

42, Owner, Saffron Table Catering, Doha, Qatar. Difficulty: intermediate.

Bank Client Discovery Simulator, 19 November 2026

Sample interview, part 2 of 3: [1. Conversation](1-conversation.md) · **2. Summary and recommendations** · [3. Coach feedback](3-coach-feedback.md)

## Student's summary

### Needs identified

Cash flow gap: his corporate clients pay in 60 to 90 days, but staff and suppliers are due within 30 days, which puts pressure on working capital.  
He only uses Sharia-compliant banking and financing.  
He plans to open a second kitchen, costing around QAR 1.5 million, mostly equipment and fit-out.  
He is open to moving his main business account if the service is better than at his current bank, where he pays fees.

### Recommended products

- Tijara Business Account
- Islamic Receivables Finance
- Ijara Equipment Finance

### Justification

Islamic Receivables Finance closes the payment gap by advancing up to 80% of his approved invoices on Sharia-compliant terms, with a fixed profit agreed upfront. He meets the requirements: over 2 years of trading and audited statements. It requires a Tijara Business Account, which also suits him as his main account: it is Sharia-compliant, handles payroll through WPS and has no monthly fee above an average balance of QAR 50,000. Ijara Equipment Finance can fund the kitchen equipment on a lease-to-own basis, but it does not cover the fit-out, so that part would need separate planning.
