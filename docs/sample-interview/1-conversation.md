## Interview

**Ahmed:** Hello, thanks for making time to see me. My catering business has been growing quickly, and I think it's time to look at what the bank can do for us.

**Student:** Thank you for coming Ahmed. Tell me a bit about the business and what made you want to look at banking options now?

**Ahmed:** I founded Saffron Table Catering eight years ago, and we now have 25 staff and about QAR 6 million in annual revenue. The growth has been fast, up 40% in two years, which is great but also changes how we handle money. I just want to find banking solutions that keep up with that pace.

**Student:** Okay, so could you tell me right now what is the biggest financial challenge you're facing in your business right now?

**Ahmed:** The main squeeze is timing. Corporate clients often take 60 to 90 days to pay invoices, but I have to pay my staff and suppliers within 30 days. Twice this year I had to delay supplier payments, and once I even used personal savings to cover payroll.

**Student:** Before I suggest anything, are there any requirements the products need to meet, for example perhaps islamic banking?

**Ahmed:** Yes, that is a firm requirement for me. I only use Sharia-compliant banking and financing. I actually turned down a conventional loan from my current bank last year specifically because it wasn't compliant.

**Student:** Alright that won't be an issue with us. Where do you see the business in the next year or two?

**Ahmed:** I plan to open a second central kitchen in Al Wakrah within the next 12 months. I need about QAR 1.5 million for that, split between equipment and fit-out.

**Student:** I see, out of that amount, Roughly what would the second kitchen cost and what would the rest of the money mainly go on?

**Ahmed:** Roughly QAR 900,000 is for the kitchen equipment, and the remaining QAR 600,000 covers the fit-out and renovation.

**Student:** Alright, based on everything you have mentioned, One option for the cash-flow gap is our Islamic Receivables Finance. It advances up to 80% of approved invoices, it's Sharia-compliant with a fixed profit agreed upfront, and it needs a Tijara Business Account with us. How does that sound?

**Ahmed:** That sounds like it could solve the timing issue, but I need to know how the profit is calculated. Since it’s fixed upfront, does the rate change if the client pays early or late? Also, what are the specific requirements for the Tijara account?

**Student:** The profit is a fixed amount agreed for each invoice when we advance the money, and it doesn't compound, so it won't grow if your client pays late. I'd want to confirm with our finance team exactly how early or late payment is handled before you commit. For the Tijara account, the business needs to be registered in Qatar with a valid Commercial Registration and trading for at least a year. There's no monthly fee if you keep an average balance of QAR 50,000, otherwise it's QAR 100 a month. It also handles your payroll through WPS, with full online banking

**Ahmed:** That works for me. I have my CR and have been trading for eight years, so I meet the requirements. I’m currently paying a fee at my other bank, so the no-fee option if I keep QAR 50,000 is appealing. How long does it take to set up the account and get the first invoice advanced?

**Student:** I don't want to promise a date before checking with our team, but here's what's involved. First we open the Tijara account with your CR. For the receivables finance we'd need your last 2 years of audited financial statements, and we'd approve the corporate clients whose invoices you want to finance. Once the limit is approved, there's a one-off arrangement fee of 0.5% of the limit, and you can then draw against approved invoices, up to 80% of each one. I'll come back to you with a realistic timeline.

**Ahmed:** That sounds manageable. I have the last three years of audited statements ready, so I can send those over immediately. I’m happy to move my main business account to you if the service is better, which it needs to be. Please let me know what the next step is.

**Student:** Great. To make sure I've got it right: your corporate clients pay in 60 to 90 days while your costs are due in 30, everything needs to be Sharia-compliant, and you're planning a second kitchen of around QAR 1.5 million. The next step is to open the Tijara account and send me your audited statements and the list of clients whose invoices you'd like to finance. I'll come back to you with the approval timeline, and we can look at financing for the kitchen equipment separately. Does that sound right?

**Ahmed:** Yes, that is exactly right. I will send over the statements and the client list today. Please keep me updated on the timeline, and let's look at the equipment financing once the account is active. Thanks for your time.
